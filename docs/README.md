# Open Codenames

**Codenames played with AI-generated images and AI teammates.**

Open Codenames is a deduction and communication game in the style of **Codenames Pictures**. The board is a 5×4 grid of pictures generated by an AI image model — by default black-and-white surreal mashups (an elephant whose trunk is an umbrella and whose tail is a propeller), so each picture can answer to several clues. Your teammate and your opponents are large language models (LLMs) that look at the pictures themselves — real players with their own memory and strategy.

## Quick start

1. Run **Open Codenames.exe**.
2. Read and accept the important information screen (it explains costs and how your keys are handled).
3. Enter at least one LLM API key (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, OpenRouter, xAI, Mistral or a local model — it must be able to see images). See [docs/API_SETUP.md](docs/API_SETUP.md).
4. Optionally enter one image-generation key (Stability AI, Black Forest Labs FLUX, OpenAI, Google, Replicate or a local Stable Diffusion). Without one, boards are dealt from the built-in **standard deck**.
5. Press **Play now**.

## How a game works

- Two teams, **Green** and **Red**, each have a **spymaster** (sees the secret key) and an **operative** (does not). On the standard 20-picture board the starting team has 8 pictures, the other 7, and 5 are neutral. There is no assassin card.
- The spymaster gives **one word and a number**: “Ocean 3” means three of the team’s pictures relate to “ocean”. The word must be about what the pictures show — not their position or letters.
- The operative picks pictures one by one — up to the number plus one. A neutral picture ends the turn and an opponent’s picture gives them the point.
- The first team to reveal all its pictures wins.

In **Quick Play** you and an AI teammate face two AI opponents. Your role alternates between spymaster and operative. Before each clue your AI teammate gives, you choose how many pictures it aims for: **Auto** (it opens with 1, 2 or 3 at 25% / 50% / 25% and then aims for as many as it needs to stay ahead) or a fixed 1–4. AI players never repeat a clue already given in the game.

After every game the **debrief** shows what each clue was meant to point at, what every AI considered, and why it chose what it did.

## Important

- **You pay the AI providers directly.** Every picture and AI move is billed to your own account. Open Codenames does not charge for or pay these costs. See [docs/COSTS.md](docs/COSTS.md).
- **Your keys stay on your computer**, encrypted with Windows DPAPI. They are sent only to the provider you chose. See [docs/PRIVACY_SECURITY.md](docs/PRIVACY_SECURITY.md).
- No account, no Open Codenames server, no telemetry.

## The standard deck

The game ships with 68 picture cards cut from black-and-white wood engravings by **J. J. Grandville** (1803–1847): animals living as people from *Scènes de la vie privée et publique des animaux* and the dreamlike inventions of *Un Autre Monde* (1844). All artwork is in the public domain (The Met Open Access and Wikimedia Commons). Open **Decks** in the top bar to browse every card with its title, artist, year and source, or see [docs/DECK_CREDITS.md](docs/DECK_CREDITS.md). You can also import your own picture collections and make one the standard — see [docs/COLLECTIONS.md](docs/COLLECTIONS.md).

Having trouble? See [docs/TROUBLESHOOTING.md](docs/TROUBLESHOOTING.md).
